# Infra-FIRE: Infrastructure Financial Independence

- Status: PUBLIC RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS / FIELD PRACTICE
- Date: 2026-10-15
- Context: ZeroRoomLab, rural resilience, open-source hardware and Patient Capital
- Definition: Proposed extension to FIRE, **not** a claim that this is an established financial-planning category or that ZeroRoomLab has achieved complete independence.

## Hypothesis

Traditional FIRE builds enough investable financial capital to make paid employment optional. **Infra-FIRE** tests another route: reduce mandatory cash transactions for essential services by owning, maintaining and reproducing the physical infrastructure that supplies them.

The key question changes from *How much financial capital buys my survival?* to *Which life-support dependencies can remain functional when cash income, local work opportunities, utility services or logistics fail?*

Money itself can become a **common-mode tail risk** when nearly every survival pathway requires the same payment interface. Cash still matters for taxes, replacement parts, fuel, medicine, logistics, liabilities and inaccessible services. The aim is not a cash-free claim but lower compulsory dependence and better failure isolation.

## Field practice, not a generic rural fantasy

ZeroRoomLab's reported field configuration includes owned land, a private drinking-water well, access to agricultural river water, and 3D-printed water-system components. The lab experiments with off-grid electricity, local computing, field repair and salvage. Rights to use river water, well ownership, water quality, safety and infrastructure maintenance have distinct evidence and legal scopes. These details should not be collapsed into one blanket claim of regulatory or resource independence.

Failures, operating expenses and externally required inputs remain part of the record. A backup supply under tight power limits is not equivalent to a fully independent production baseload.

## Capital classes

| Capital | Example | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Financial | cash reserves, liquid investments | procurement and emergency options |
| Physical | land, well, pipes, batteries, tools | direct capacity to provide essential services |
| Access | lawful water/resource access, shared rights | continuity and permitted use of resources |
| Reproductive | designs, fabrication, testing, repair know-how | ability to restore function after breakage |
| Commons | OSH files, manuals, test receipts and forks | others' ability to reproduce and improve systems |
| Social | mutual aid, specialist collaboration | repair and recovery pathways beyond the household |

## Evaluation: test independence instead of declaring it

Measure each critical service (water, power, heat, communications, food, sanitation, fabrication) using:

1. Minimum recurring external cash outlay and replacement reserve.
2. Days of service without new cash income, grid service or external deliveries.
3. Single points of failure and correlated shocks (winter, flooding, illness, parts shortage).
4. Repairability: locally reproducible parts, tools, time and skill requirements.
5. Quality and safety evidence appropriate to each service.
6. Total lifecycle burden including labor, permits, taxes, loss, and decommissioning.

An illustrative test objective is to reduce `P(essential_service_failure | cash_inflow=0)` while not shifting unmeasured risk onto other people or the environment.

## OSH publication and sponsor funding

The next concrete output is a reproducible OSH archive for eligible 3D-printed water-system parts: editable CAD, STL/3MF, material/process settings, installation context, operating limits, maintenance observations, test receipts and revision history.

Release in stages: inventory -> source-design cleanup -> bench verification -> field-test documentation -> public release. **A printed part used in a drinking-water installation is not automatically certified for potable-water contact or pressure service**. Material suitability and hygienic risk need specific testing and labeling; unverified parts can still be documented as experimental or non-potable designs.

### Funding milestone (target, not guaranteed release)

**$1,000/month in recurring GitHub Sponsors support** is a proposed funding gate for durable OSH archiving, Git LFS or equivalent large-file storage and delivery, bandwidth, testing and publication work. Reaching this funding target would permit allocation and planning; it does not automatically unlock Git LFS or guarantee any particular deliverable. GitHub Free currently includes a limited Git LFS quota, with additional usage billed separately. Staged work remains resource-gated.

The intended cycle:

```text
patient / non-exclusive support
   -> publication infrastructure + test and fabrication capacity
   -> reusable OSH designs and evidence
   -> locally repairable survival systems
   -> lower mandatory cash dependency and reusable public knowledge
```

No sponsorship buys upstream control, exclusive rights or guaranteed output. This follows the existing Non-Exclusive Seed Steward and licensing policies. Different CAD, text, software and third-party assets retain their documented license boundaries.


## Legal, credit and payment-rail bottleneck (funding requirement)

The financial/legal constraints affecting the lab are also an **infrastructure dependency**. Usage-billed Git LFS or hosted storage can require a supported payment method and continued account access. Buying capacity and having the lawful, creditworthy means to pay for it are distinct gates.

The founder reports unresolved legacy legal and credit issues, with a **rough order-of-magnitude exposure of US$200,000** mentioned for legal/financial restructuring. This is a founder-reported planning figure, not an audited balance, verified settlement sum, or claim that paying that amount would automatically restore credit-card eligibility. Legal counsel would need to determine the obligations, creditor and guarantor positions, settlement alternatives, time limits, legal entity boundaries, the actual budget, and potential payment routes. We do not request a funder to assume unknown liabilities or treat a sponsorship as a debt investment.

Consequently, non-exclusive legal counsel, accounting support, lawful debt/credit restructuring and reliable payment rails are legitimate **research infrastructure funding needs**, separate from the $1,000/month recurring OSH publishing milestone. Where a card-based billing route is not available, fixed-price/prepaid storage, third-party institutional hosting under appropriate agreements, self-hosting or donated capacity are additional branches to evaluate. No bypass of identity, platform billing, or financial compliance requirements is proposed.

## Research position

Infra-FIRE is a proposed **systems-engineering extension of FIRE**, not a replacement for conventional financial independence, proof of achieved FIRE, or an established financial product. It connects ZeroRoomLab's Portable Civilization concept to a measurable terrestrial deployment target: a livable, repairable, forkable rural infrastructure stack.
